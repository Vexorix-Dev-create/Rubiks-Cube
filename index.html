The strip on the right isn't decoration. It's a one-dimensional automaton — a single local rule, executed on repeat, producing a pattern you never explicitly wrote.

02 / SELF
OPERATOR — ZYREX

I think in systems before I think in features.

Before I write a single line of code, I ask: what's the smallest unit here, and what happens when it repeats a thousand times? Most critical bugs aren't born from broken logic — they're born from the assumption that a system will always behave the way it did the first time it was tested.

So I design for disturbance by default. Try clicking and dragging inside the panel on the right. The nodes will scatter for a moment — then re-organize on their own. Not because I wrote code to force them back, but because the rules they follow naturally converge toward equilibrium. That's the same principle I bring to production: engineer the right rules, not an endless list of edge cases.

Off-screen, I'm at my most focused when the systems I've built are already running on their own — when I can stop watching them line by line.
